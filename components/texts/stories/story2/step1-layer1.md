---
title: How did the Beagle voyage shape Darwin’s thinking?
---

During the HMS Beagle voyage (1831–1836), Darwin collected specimens, compared environments, and observed geological formations firsthand. He read Charles Lyell’s [[term:Principles-of-Geology]] on board, which presented Darwin with Lyells theory of [[term:Gradualism]] which emphasized a slow change over millenias of time.

This concept, along with exposure, examinations, and collecting various speciems to examine such as Finches pushed him to consider variation, adaptation, and the possibility that species were not fixed. The voyage provided the raw observations that later fueled his theoretical work...
