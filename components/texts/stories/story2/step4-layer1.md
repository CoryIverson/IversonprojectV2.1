---
title: Alfred Wallace 
---
Alfred Russel Wallace (1823–1913) was a British naturalist who spent years collecting and studying species across the world. Unlike Darwin, who came from a afluent family and upbringing, Wallace grew up with fewer resources and built his scientific career through fieldwork and specimen collecting. His observations of geographic distribution and variation led him to ideas strikingly similar to Darwin’s.

 When Wallace sent his manuscript on natural selection to Darwin in 1858, Darwin was stunned by how closely it mirrored his own, long-unpublished theory. Unsure how to proceed without wronging either man, Darwin turned to his friend and colleagues Charles Lyell and Joseph Hooker. They organized a joint presentation at the Linnean Society, where both Wallace’s paper and excerpts from Darwin’s earlier writings were read together. Thomas Henry Huxley supported this solution, which allowed Darwin and Wallace to be recognized simultaneously without direct rivalry. Although the Linnean Society presentation effectively made them co-authors of natural selection, Wallace never contested Darwin’s priority

Wallace’s manuscript forced Darwin to publish On the Origin of Species far sooner than he planned, crystallizing years of thought, notes and rough theories into a coherent book.
