---
title: The public persona versus the private reality
---

Publicly: Huxley rather famously called himself and historically is given the nickname "Darwin's Bulldog," stating he was ready to fight critics who were "barking" at the theory of evolution.[^1] He framed the Origin of Species not just as a biology book, but as a scientific revolution, giving his own (anonymous) positive review, but often publicly engaging with critiques of the book and evoluitonary theory as a whole. His authority came in part from not just his vigor but his private connections and institutional status.

Privately: Behind the scenes, Huxley legitimized these ideas through institutional power and a large social network. He held top positions in the Royal, Geological, and Linnean Societies and created or was a member of various other social clubs with infleuntiual figures.[^2] This status allowed Huxley and others to boost or halter theories from the likes of Darwin, other naturalist, and science in general from the top down by utilizing his the growing authority of institituions and his vast social and correspondence networks.

[^1]: Darwin, Francis, Charles Darwin, and Thomas Henry Huxley. The Life and Letters of Charles Darwin, Including an Autobiographical Chapter. London: John Murray, 1887.
[^2]: For more information, see Desmond, Adrian J. Huxley : The Devil’s Disciple. London: M. Joseph, 1994.
