Principles of Geology (1830–1833) by Charles Lyell’s was a extremely influential three-volume work arguing that Earth’s landscapes were shaped by slow, continuous processes acting over immense spans of time. Lyell rejected catastrophic explanations and instead emphasized “uniformitarian” change where the present is the key to understanding the past. His ideas provided the temporal and conceptual framework that deeply shaped many scientific theories about nature at the time.
